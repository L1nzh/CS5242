Here is a summary of my current baseline approach and my strategic plan for the next iterations:
Task-Specific Features & Progressive Modeling
Based on my feature analysis, the pet segmentation task has two distinct challenges: capturing the global pose/shape and distinguishing the fuzzy, high-frequency boundaries (e.g., fur blending into the background). To address this, my strategy is progressive:
Phase 1 (Current Baseline): I am starting with a binary classification approach (Foreground vs. Background) to ensure the model successfully learns the global shapes.
Phase 2 (Next Step): I will shift to a 3-class segmentation (Foreground, Background, and Boundary Contour). This explicitly forces the network to focus on the hardest visual features—the transition areas.
Justification for a Custom U-Net Architecture
To maintain the flexibility needed for the transition from Phase 1 to Phase 2, I decided to build the U-Net architecture from scratch rather than relying on a rigid, pre-trained black-box model. This "hand-built" baseline gives me the architectural freedom to inject specific modules later—such as Attention Gates in the skip connections—to better capture those difficult boundary features once the 3-class task is introduced.
Dataset Augmentation and Improvement
Regarding dataset collection, before blindly scraping external data, my immediate approach is to heavily rely on the Albumentations pipeline. I am applying geometric transforms, blur, and RGB shifts to simulate real-world variations (e.g., varying depth-of-field and lighting). Once I evaluate the baseline's failure cases (e.g., pets camouflaged in specific environments), I will use those insights to collect highly targeted external samples that address the model's specific blind spots.
Comprehensive Evaluation Metrics
To accurately measure improvements, especially when dealing with the highly imbalanced "boundary" class in Phase 2, my evaluation goes beyond standard Loss. I am tracking:
Mean IoU for overall region overlap.
Generalized Dice Score to specifically penalize errors on minority pixels (like thin tails or edge contours), ensuring the model is truly learning the fine details.
